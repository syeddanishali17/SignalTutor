**Picture it.** A wire is full of electric **charge** (electrons) that can move. A battery pushes on all of it at once, so the charge starts to circulate — like a bicycle chain, not like a stream of individual runners.

**What it is.** The **current** is the amount of charge passing one point per second:

$$I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} \qquad 1\,\text{A} = 1\,\tfrac{\text{C}}{\text{s}}$$

Three facts you will use in every circuit:

1. **It needs a closed loop.** Open the loop anywhere and the current drops to zero *everywhere*, instantly.
2. **In a single loop it is the same everywhere.** Nothing is used up. Ammeters at three places read the same.
3. **Direction is a convention.** We draw the arrow from the battery's + side, through the circuit, back to −. (Electrons actually drift the other way; it does not matter for any calculation here.)

**In the app.** Current is drawn as **blue moving dashes with arrowheads**: faster and thicker dashes = more amperes. The dashes picture the *rate*, not individual electrons.

**Sizes.** Sensor circuits work with mA (milliamperes) and µA (microamperes).
