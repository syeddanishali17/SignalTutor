Because voltage is a *difference*, we need one agreed point to measure everything from. That node is **ground**, drawn as ⏚ (three shrinking lines), and defined as $0\,\text{V}$.

Once ground is chosen, "node A is at 3 V" means "3 V measured from ground to A".

**Move ground to another node** and

* every node label shifts by the same amount (the new ground reads 0 V),
* every **difference** between two nodes is unchanged,
* every current is unchanged — the circuit does not notice.

In bridge and amplifier drawings, ground is usually the bottom line. **Find it first** when you read any diagram.
