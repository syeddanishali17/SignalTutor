**Picture it.** Every point in a circuit sits at some electrical "height" called its **potential** $\varphi$ (Greek "phi"). A **node** is a connection point: every point joined by plain wire is one node with one potential.

**What it is.** A **voltage** is the *difference* between two potentials:

$$V_{AB} = \varphi_A - \varphi_B$$

That is why a **voltmeter** always has **two leads**: it can only compare two points. "Node A is at 5 V" is shorthand for "5 V measured from the chosen zero point (ground) to A".

**Consequences you will use all the time**

* If **both** nodes rise by 2 V, $V_{AB}$ does not change. (That shared part will later be called **common mode** — the key to the Wheatstone bridge and to amplifiers.)
* $V_{BA} = -V_{AB}$: swapping the leads flips the sign.
* Choosing a different zero point relabels every node but changes no difference.

**In the app.** Potentials are drawn as heights on a scale; a voltage is a **bracket spanning two points**, with its signed value.
