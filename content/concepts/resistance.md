**Picture it.** A **resistor** is a deliberately "narrow" part of the circuit. The same push gets less current through a narrower part; the charge that does get through loses energy as heat.

**What it is.**

$$R = \frac{V}{I} \qquad 1\,\Omega = 1\,\tfrac{\text{V}}{\text{A}}$$

For a wire or film of length $l$, cross-section $A$ and material constant $\rho$ (resistivity): $R = \rho\,\dfrac{l}{A}$. **Stretch it** (longer, thinner) and $R$ rises — this is why **strain changes resistance**, the idea behind every strain gauge. In silicon even $\rho$ changes strongly with strain (the *piezoresistive* effect), which makes silicon gauges very sensitive.

**Symbols in this app.** A plain rectangle is a fixed resistor (the European/IEC symbol; some drawings use a zig-zag — same part). The component's **name** ($R_1$) is printed in bold next to it; its **value** ($350\,\Omega$ or $R + \Delta R$) sits in a separate tag. A diagonal arrow through the rectangle marks a **strain gauge** — a resistor whose value changes (see the legend under the bridge).
