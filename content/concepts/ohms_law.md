$$I = \frac{\overbrace{V}^{\text{push}}}{\underbrace{R}_{\text{opposition}}}$$

* $V$ up → $I$ up in proportion: double the push, double the current.
* $R$ up → $I$ down: double the resistance, half the current.

**Use it per part.** $V$ is the voltage *across that one resistor*, $I$ the current *through it*.

**On a graph.** Plot $I$ against $V$ for one resistor and you get a straight line through the origin with slope $1/R$. A steeper line means a smaller resistance.

**Worked example.** $9\,\text{V}$ across $4.7\,\text{k}\Omega$:

$$I = \frac{9\,\text{V}}{4700\,\Omega} = 0.00191\,\text{A} = 1.91\,\text{mA}$$
