Two wires carry voltages $V_1$ and $V_2$, both measured from ground. Their **difference** is the differential signal:

$$V_d = V_2 - V_1$$

When the two wires move in **opposite directions** around a middle level, only $V_d$ changes.

A Wheatstone bridge outputs exactly such a signal: both nodes sit around $V_0/2$, and the strain pushes them a few millivolts apart. All the information is in $V_2 - V_1$.

Measuring a few mV of difference while both wires sit at 2.5 V — and while mains interference couples equally into both — is the central problem of the amplifier lessons.
