Almost every formula in this course is a fraction, so it pays to read one fluently.

$$\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}} \qquad\text{e.g.}\qquad I = \frac{\overbrace{V}^{\text{numerator: the push}}}{\underbrace{R}_{\text{denominator: the opposition}}}$$

* **Numerator up → result up** (in proportion). Double $V$, double $I$.
* **Denominator up → result down.** Double $R$, half $I$.
* A denominator that is a **sum**, like $R_1 + R_2$, means "the total". The current through two resistors in a row is $\dfrac{V}{R_1 + R_2}$. The push divided by the *total* opposition.
* A fraction of two things with the **same unit** (like $\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$) has **no unit** — it is a pure share, a number between 0 and 1.

**In the app:** in Beginner mode the equation strip labels numerators and denominators the first time they matter.
