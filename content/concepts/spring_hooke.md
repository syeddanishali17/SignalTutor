A spring resists being squeezed. **Hooke's law**: the restoring force grows in proportion to the displacement,

$$F_\text{spring} = k\,x$$

* $x$ — how far the spring is compressed (m)
* $k$ — the **spring constant** or stiffness (N/m): a stiff spring has a large $k$

**The lecture's membrane model.** The membrane is held by two springs, each of stiffness $k$. Pressure pushes it down by $x$ until the springs push back just as hard:

$$\underbrace{2k\,x}_{\text{springs push up}} = \underbrace{p\,A}_{\text{pressure pushes down}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \frac{A}{2k}\,p$$

So the **transfer function** pressure → displacement is $H_{p\text{-}x} = \dfrac{x}{p} = \dfrac{A}{2k}$ — a constant: doubling $p$ doubles $x$. (A real membrane is a bending plate; the spring picture keeps the physics and the maths simple.)
