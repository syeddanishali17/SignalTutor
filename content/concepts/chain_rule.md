The capacitance does not depend on time *directly* — it depends on the gap, and the gap moves:

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = \underbrace{\frac{dC}{dx}}_{\text{F per metre}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\frac{dx}{dt}}_{\text{metres per second}}$$

The units chain like fractions: $\dfrac{\text{F}}{\text{m}}\cdot\dfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \dfrac{\text{F}}{\text{s}}$.

* $\dfrac{dC}{dx}$. The **slope** of the $C(x)$ curve: how much $C$ changes per metre of movement. For parallel plates $\dfrac{dC}{dx} = \dfrac{\varepsilon A}{(d-x)^2}$.
* $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. The membrane's **speed**. Sound makes it oscillate quickly, so this is large; a slow pressure drift makes it nearly zero.
