Every meter or amplifier input behaves like a resistor $R_\text{in}$ from the input to ground (or between its two inputs). By Ohm's law it draws

$$I_\text{in} = \frac{V}{R_\text{in}}$$

* **High** $R_\text{in}$ (megaohms or more): almost no current, the source is undisturbed. *Good for measuring voltages.*
* **Low** $R_\text{in}$: the input **loads** the source — you saw this in the divider and in the bridge, where the output dropped.

"Impedance" is the general word that also covers AC behaviour (capacitors); for the circuits here it simply means the input resistance.

**Ideal op-amp inputs** draw *no* current at all (infinite input impedance) — one of the five ideal properties.
