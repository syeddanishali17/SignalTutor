The symbol $\Delta$ (Greek capital "delta") means **"change in"**: $\Delta R$ is the change in resistance.

A change on its own says little. Is $\Delta R = 1\,\Omega$ big? On a 10 Ω resistor yes (10 %), on a 1 kΩ resistor no (0.1 %). So we **normalise** — divide the change by the original value:

$$\frac{\Delta R}{R} = \frac{1\,\Omega}{1000\,\Omega} = 10^{-3} = 0.1\,\%$$

Normalised quantities have **no unit** (Ω divided by Ω). That is why the bridge formulas are written with $\Delta R/R$: the answer does not care whether the gauge is 120 Ω or 1 kΩ.

The same idea appears as **strain** $\varepsilon = \Delta L/L$, and later as $x/d$ for a capacitor gap.
