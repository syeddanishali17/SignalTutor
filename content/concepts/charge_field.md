Connect two metal plates, facing each other across a small gap, to a voltage $V$. Charge flows **for a moment**: the source pushes electrons off one plate and onto the other. Then the flow stops.

* one plate carries $+Q$, the other exactly $-Q$ (charge is moved, not created);
* between the plates there is an **electric field** — drawn as lines from $+$ to $-$;
* **no charge crosses the gap**: it is an insulator (air or vacuum).

**Opposite charges attract.** The two plates pull on each other with a force that grows quickly as they get closer — this is what causes **pull-in**:

$$F_\text{el} = \frac{\varepsilon A\,V^2}{2\,(d - x)^2}$$

(supplemental: the standard parallel-plate result). Halving the gap makes this force **four times** larger.
