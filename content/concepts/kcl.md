Charge cannot pile up in a junction of wires, so at every **node**:

$$\sum I_\text{in} = \sum I_\text{out}$$

**Example.** 5 mA flows into a node and 2 mA leaves through one branch ⇒ 3 mA must leave through the other.

**Arrows are guesses.** Draw each current's arrow any way you like. If the result comes out **negative**, the current really flows the other way. Let the sign tell you the truth.

**Where you will need it.** In the bridge derivation (the meter draws no current, so each branch keeps its own current), and in the instrumentation amplifier, where *the same current* runs through $R_2$, $R_1$ and $R_2$, because no current can leave into the amplifier inputs.
