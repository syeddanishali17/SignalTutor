Forces balancing is not enough — ask what happens after a small nudge.

* **Stable** (ball in a bowl): move it a little and the net force points **back**. It returns.
* **Unstable** (ball on a hilltop): the forces also balance exactly at the top, but any nudge creates a net force **away**. It runs off.

**Test with slopes.** For the membrane: a spring pushes back with $k_\text{eff}\,x$, the plates pull with $F_\text{el}(x)$. Nudge it by $\delta x$ toward the plate. The spring force grows by $k_\text{eff}\,\delta x$, the pull grows by $\frac{dF_\text{el}}{dx}\,\delta x$.

$$\text{stable} \iff k_\text{eff} > \frac{dF_\text{el}}{dx}$$

— the spring must stiffen faster than the attraction grows.
